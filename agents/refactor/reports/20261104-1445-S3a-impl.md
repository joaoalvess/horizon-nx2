## What the diff does

1. **The tap.** The encode thread hands each 256-record block to `rec_tap` before `rec_exec` runs it.
   - The tap is compiled only where `NVN_REC_TAP` is defined: `nvn_replay.m` and `t_rec_digest.m`. The host never defines it.
   - It digests only between `rdg_reset` and `rdg_final`, through `g_rdg_live`, which it checks once per block.
2. **The test sink.** `nvn_rec_digest.inc` hashes every record (op and arguments) into one SHA-256, following a per-op layout table (`g_rdg_ops`). An op with no layout is fatal.
   - **Objects** are named by the order in which they first appear in the frame's calls. The name is stored in an association, scoped to the epoch, so a new object at a freed address gets a new name.
   - **Object descriptions** are hashed once, at first naming:
     - a texture: type, format, size, levels, layers, samples, usage, storage, swizzle, and parent or buffer;
     - a buffer: length and storage;
     - a pipeline, depth-stencil state or allocator: its label;
     - a heap or counter heap: size and type.
   - **GPU addresses** become the name of the resident buffer they lie in, plus the offset into it. The sorted table is rebuilt from `g_res_objects` on a miss, at most once per block.
   - **Render pass descriptors** are hashed by their attachments, actions, clear values, sizes, visibility buffer and rate map.
   - **Normalised values:**
     - event values, including the sparse map's, are taken relative to the event's first value in the frame;
     - the `ubc` sequence of a `COMMIT` is taken relative to `ubc_head()` at the frame's start;
     - texture and sampler resource IDs are replaced by a counted marker;
     - a note record is hashed without its host-internal counter.
3. **The replayer.** `nvn_replay --digest` does the following:
   - sets `g_rec = 1`, so the recorder is on;
   - runs the replay layer's background `dispatch_async` work inline (see the dk_ypunch section);
   - calls `rdg_reset` after each replay's region restore and `rdg_final` after its last `mtl_finish`;
   - prints `replay n: command digest <sha256>: N calls, N objects, N GPU addresses (N in no resident buffer), N texture or sampler IDs not named`.

   Related options:
   - with `--repeat n`, each child appends its digest to a temporary file, and the parent prints `command digest: n of n fresh replays give <hex>`, or a `[RED]` line listing the distinct digests (exit 1);
   - `--digest-expect <hex>` compares each replay against a known digest (exit 1 on a mismatch);
   - `--digest-dump` writes each replay's canonical call list as text, `<capture>.replay<n>.calls.txt`.

## The plan's sink requirements

- **Chosen at build time or once per submission, never per call.**
  - At build time: `NVN_REC_TAP`, which the host never defines.
  - Once per replayed frame: `rdg_reset` / `rdg_final` switch the sink, and `rec_tap` checks the switch once per block.
- **Liskov: the test sink keeps the semantics the translator relies on.** The test sink is a tap in front of the recorder's executor. Every call still reaches Metal through `rec_exec`, in the same FIFO order, through the same stand-ins.
  - **Encoders:** the recorder still makes a fresh `DmRecEnc` per encoder, so the `g_enc != en` checks are unchanged.
  - **Lifetimes:** the tap reads a block before `rec_exec` releases its retained objects.
    - It holds no strong reference beyond the block's autorelease pool.
    - The table of buffers is weak (`NSPointerArray`), and a hit is checked through `gpuAddress`.
    - The rebuild copies `allObjects` inside its own pool.
  - **Proven by:** `t_rec_digest`, which reads buffer bytes and a clear pixel back after the recorder runs; and on the corpus, the recorder and test-sink pixels against the Metal-sink pixels, below.

## Digest stability per capture, and the root cause on dk_ypunch

**First corpus run, before the shim.** 12 of the 13 captures gave one digest in 8 of 8 fresh replays. dk_ypunch gave 3 digests in 8 replays:
- 6 replays of 5095 calls and 127 passes;
- 1 of 9306 calls and 314 passes;
- 1 of 8335 calls and 271 passes.

The same spread showed without the recorder: DK's Metal-sink replays had 127, 303, 257 and 127 passes. So the varying passes come neither from the recorder nor from the sink.

**Cause.** `sm_amp_get` (`nvn_mtl.m` 7177) builds the amplified pipeline of a slice-merged pass in the background, with `dispatch_async` on a global queue, on first use. The frame never waits for it: `sm_plan` 8735 and `draw` 10554. In a fresh process, dk_ypunch's first slice-merge candidate asks for one such pipeline.
- Usually the build does not land within the frame, which keeps its 127 passes, with 1 draw counted as waiting in `g_sm_nwait`.
- When it lands mid-frame, later passes merge. Their draws then find their own amplified pipelines missing, and the pass splits at each draw: 83 and 95 waits, 271-314 passes.

Pixels stay within noise either way.

**First attempt, rejected and reverted.** I first waited for the background builds and digested the second replay (`--warm 2`), marking the first as "unsettled". The second replay was still not deterministic: 2 digests in 8 processes, 203 against 204 objects. Each variant followed what that process's first replay had done. The long first replay leaves the argument arena with one more 4 MB block, and the next replay's binding resolves to it. That code is not in the diff.

**The fix.**
- **The shim.** Under `--digest`, the replayer runs the translator's `dispatch_async` blocks at once, on the thread that dispatches them. This is a macro shim in `nvn_replay.m` (`rdg_dispatch_async`, `g_rdg_sync`), in the style of its existing `nvn_game_fps` and `g_ft` macro shims. `nvn_mtl.m` itself is not changed.
- **What it covers:** amplified pipelines, mesh pipelines (`nvn_geometry.inc` 423), speculative and demanded builds (`pso_pend_start`), and the shader prefetch (10817).
- **Deadlock risk:** every site releases its locks before dispatching, and none dispatches to the main queue. The hybrid stand-ins (6943) are off in captures, and 6593 is iOS only.
- **What the digest becomes:** that of a translation whose background builds take no time. The draw that first asks for such a pipeline still goes without it.
- **Result:** dk_ypunch gives one digest in 12 of 12 fresh processes, and in 8 of 8 in the final corpus run. It is the common 127-pass digest.
- **Plain replays** (without `--digest`) dispatch asynchronously as before.
- **Unchanged by the shim:** the other 12 captures give the same digests as before it.

## Decisions that belong to the user, with the conservative choice taken

1. **"Created in order" is read as first appearance in the frame's calls.** Objects made before the frame (captured textures, pipelines and allocators from earlier frames) are named when the frame first uses them. This needs no hook at object creation in `nvn_mtl.m`.
2. **Texture and sampler resource IDs are a counted marker, not resolved to their object.** Metal has no lookup from `MTLResourceID` to its object. They come from `copy_t2t_draw` and `draw_tex`: 4 per DK frame, none on Pokopia.
3. **`--digest` forces the recorder on for every game**, Pokopia included, whose Mac default is the Metal sink. The digest is always of the recorder's stream. Pokopia's Metal sink is judged by the plain replays.
4. **Under `--digest`, the translator's background dispatches run inline.** The digest is that of a translation whose background builds take no time.

   Alternatives the user may prefer:
   - a translator-side deterministic-build switch in S3b, which may touch `nvn_mtl.m`;
   - accepting timing-dependent digests on frames with background pipelines (dk_ypunch now, and mesh frames in general).

## Blind spots (documented in `docs/development.md`)

- what the CPU writes into buffers: argument blocks, uniform shadows, upload staging;
- the upload batches' own copies (only their commits);
- which functions a pipeline holds: the games' pipelines carry no label, so a pipeline is known only by where it first appears;
- residency calls;
- the present;
- command buffers made outside the recorder (`q_cb`).

The pixels still judge all of these.

Also:
- a `--warm` replay may give another digest than the first;
- `--repeat` still does not pass `--warm` to its children.
