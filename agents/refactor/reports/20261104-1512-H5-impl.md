## Root cause

HEAD line numbers, `dm/host/hle_thread.c`.

`dm_yield_to_core_peers` (`:194-206`) runs on a waker pinned to its core, from `svc_thread` (`:950`). This happens after `cv_flush` (`:944`), when the SVC woke a higher-priority same-core thread (`yield_pending`), or after every thread SVC in all-peers mode (`g_core_preempt`).

Under `G` it looks for a peer that is `started`, not `exited`, not `host_owned`, pinned to the caller's core (`mask == bit`), higher priority, `thr_runnable` (`:134-136`: no `wkind`, not `sleeping`, no `native_wait`, no `pause_req`, `*host_work` 0), and either `all` or `thr_in_handoff` (`:138-141`). If it finds one, it releases `G`, sleeps 50 µs with `nanosleep` (`:205`), and scans again.

Nothing tells it that the peer has stopped holding the core, so the waker learns of it only at its next scan:
- On this Mac a 50 µs `nanosleep` returns after 63-77 µs (timer slack).
- In the microbenchmark, a woken thread that blocks at once frees its waker after 63.5 µs (median, HEAD), when the handoff itself is over in about 2 µs.
- A handoff of length L costs L rounded up to the next poll.

What ends the wait, and where (HEAD):

| Change | Field | Under `G` | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| The peer blocks (any guest wait) | `wkind`, then `hand_leave` | yes | `block` `:273-294` (`hand_leave` `:276`) |
| The peer sleeps or yields to any thread | `hand_leave` when it holds handoffs; `sleeping` | `hand_leave` yes; `sleeping` no | SleepThread `:994`; `yield_for` (`:790-`), `dm_guest_sleep_ns` (`:931-`) |
| The peer enters a native wait | `native_wait`, `hand_leave` | yes | `dm_thread_native_wait(1)` `:180` |
| The peer makes a service request | `hand_leave` | yes | `dm_thread_service_request` `:158` |
| The peer exits | `exited` | yes | `thread_exit` `:384` |
| The priority of the peer or of the waiter changes | `prio` | yes | 0x0D `:1005` |
| Another thread pauses the peer | `pause_req` | yes | 0x32 `:1026` |
| The peer is started again, so its handoffs are reset | `nhand` | yes | `note_runnable` `:249` |
| The peer enters host GPU work or a contended host lock | `*host_work` | no | `gfx_enter` / submit (`dm/gfx/nvn/hle_nvn.c:1258-1259`, `:1280`), `dm_host_mutex_lock` / `dm_host_thread_join` (`dm/host/dm.h:13-28`) |
| The peer becomes host-owned | `host_owned` | no | `dm_thread_mark_host` `:131` |
| The peer's core or mask changes | `core`, `mask` | no | 0x0F `:1009-1015` |

How long the waits last in practice. Handoff totals appear only in state dumps (`[dm] same-core handoffs N, total T ms`, `:699`):
- MKW `0400C3F00006E000-work/run2/b62.log`, at a `DM_CTL` dump 922-924 s into the run: 26,545 handoffs, 3,460.1 ms in total. That is 28.7 a second (the catalog says ~13), with a mean of 130 µs.
- MKW `run/b02-b04.log`, boot only: 5 handoffs in 1.1 ms, 4 in 1.7 ms, 130 in 593.5 ms (mean 4.6 ms).
- Pokopia `0400A3A01EE4C000_build/run/boot*.log` and `host-opt/round2/shaders/passlog.txt`: 3,578-3,723 handoffs per boot, in 217-358 ms (mean 58-96 µs). For example, 3,578 in 217.0 ms at about 596 s is about 6 a second.
- DK: no dump with the line was found.

A mean of 58-96 µs against a 63-77 µs poll means most of these handoffs end within the first poll. Their cost is the poll's granularity, not the work.

What a correct event-driven wait needs:
- Every change in the table that ends the wait has to wake the waiter.
- No wakeup can be lost between the scan and the wait.
- H3's deferred signals and its invariant (no path takes `G` between an unlock and its `cv_flush`) have to keep holding.
- The changes made without `G` cannot be paired with a condition variable on `G` without editing `dm/gfx` and `dm/host/dm.h`. That is outside this item's scope. For them, the bounded re-check stays.

## What the diff does

Patched line numbers.

1. New state:
   - `thr_t.hand_wait` (`:63`) marks a thread that is inside the wait.
   - `g_hand_cv` (`:101`) is the condition variable the waiters wait on, with `G` as its mutex.
   - `g_hand_waiters` (`:102`) counts waiters. All three are read and written only under `G`.
2. The wait (`:215`). When the scan finds a busy peer, the waiter sets `hand_wait`, increments `g_hand_waiters`, and calls `pthread_cond_timedwait_relative_np(&g_hand_cv, &G, 50 µs)`. It still holds `G` from the scan. It then clears `hand_wait` and decrements `g_hand_waiters`, releases `G`, and scans again. The scan and the wait share one hold of `G`, so a change made under `G` either happens before the scan (which then sees it) or after the waiter is on the condition variable (which the broadcast then reaches). The 50 µs bound stays as the re-check for the writes made without `G`, so nothing is seen later than with HEAD's poll.
3. `hand_wake(t)` (`:145-153`), under `G`, is called where `t`'s state changes in a way that can end a wait. It broadcasts `g_hand_cv` only when `g_hand_waiters` is non-zero and one of these holds:
   - in all-peers mode, any change does;
   - `t` is itself a waiter (its own priority changed);
   - `t` is in the live handoff of a waiting `w`, with the same rule as `thr_in_handoff` (`hand[i].by == w`, `epoch == w->hand_epoch`), and is higher priority than `w` and pinned to `w`'s core.

   Unrelated blocks, such as threads on other cores, lower-priority threads, or threads not woken by the waiter, do not broadcast. The test checks this: exactly one wait.
4. Call sites, all already under `G`:
   - `hand_leave` (`:155`), which covers blocking, sleeping or yielding to any thread with handoffs held, native waits and service requests;
   - `note_runnable`'s reset of `nhand` (`:262`), for a restart;
   - `thread_exit` after `exited = 1` (`:397`);
   - SetThreadPriority 0x0D before the new priority is written (`:1018`), so that the filter tests the relation as it was while the wait held;
   - SetThreadActivity 0x32 after `pause_req = 1` (`:1039`).

Unchanged:
- the scan's condition;
- every handoff rule (`note_runnable`'s records, `hand_epoch`, `yield_pending`, the mask exclusion, `host_owned`);
- `dm_thread_service_request`;
- all-peers mode's semantics.

No new `G` section is added, and `hand_wake` queues nothing on `cv_pend`, so H3's invariant and `thr_free`'s wait are untouched. The waiter enters the wait after `svc_thread`'s `cv_flush`, so it holds no deferred signals.

Design decisions:
- **The broadcast is issued under `G`, not deferred through `cv_flush`.** Its most common caller is `block`, which waits on its own condition variable right after `hand_leave`, so there is no point after the unlock to defer it to. The other callers are rare. The cost is on the thread that changes state: about 1 µs and 5k instructions per handoff, measured below.
- **A condition variable on `G`, not `os_sync_wait_on_address`.** I built a prototype with `os_sync_wait_on_address_with_timeout` on a per-waiter word (`src-H5-bench/new2/`; available since the minimum macOS is 26.6 and iOS is 26.0). On a quiet run it saved only about 1k instructions per handoff on each side (S 15.6k against 16.1k, W 15.2k against 16.3k), at the same latency. The monitor pattern on `G` is the standard one, and TSan understands it: `pthread_cond_timedwait_relative_np` is intercepted (checked with `nm`).
- **A broadcast, not a signal.** More than one waker can wait at once (one per core, or two on one core in a chain). A signal could reach the wrong one.
- **The 50 µs re-check stays.** See the writes made without `G` in the table above, and the open items.

M3 to M6 are caught only in handoff mode. In all-peers mode every `hand_wake` broadcasts (`g_core_preempt`), so the helper's own block or exit ends the wait as well. That mode needs no filter, and its semantics are unchanged. M9 was not caught before the scan's window case was added (see the test description below).

The new test, `t_handoff_wake`:
- It includes `hle_thread.c` with `pthread_cond_timedwait_relative_np` wrapped. Its default mode makes the wait untimed (`pthread_cond_wait`), so a lost wakeup hangs, and `alarm(20)` turns the hang into `[RED] t_handoff_wake: <scenario>: timed out; a same-core handoff wait was never woken` and exit 1.
- S is the main guest thread (priority 44, core 0). In each case the peer waits until S is inside the wait (`hand_wait`) before it acts, so the wakeup, not the first scan, is what is tested.
- Cases:
  - the woken thread blocks on an event, blocks on an address, sleeps*, yields to any thread*, waits for a host lock (native wait), makes a service request*, or exits;
  - another thread pauses it, lowers its priority to the waiter's, raises the waiter's to its own, or starts it again*;
  - chain: a worker woken by the woken dispatcher blocks after the dispatcher (two waits);
  - the scan's window: the woken thread blocks as soon as the waiter's scan lets go of `G` (details after this list);
  - host work entered without `G`, timed mode: it ends at the re-check, with at least one timeout, and the peer is still in host work when S returns;
  - unrelated threads* (another core, lower priority, not woken) block during the wait: exactly one wait;
  - stress: 2000 rounds in which the peer blocks right after its wake, with a second signaller on the core*.
- `*` marks handoff mode only. `--all-peers` (`DM_PREEMPT`) runs the rest: 16 cases in handoff mode, 11 in all-peers mode.

The scan's window, in detail:
- For this case the test also wraps `pthread_mutex_lock` and `pthread_mutex_unlock` of `G`, for S only.
- The unlock wrapper fires when S unlocks `G` after a lock of it with no wait in between, while S has `yield_pending` and not `hand_wait`, and the peer is runnable, in S's handoff, and already signalled (`cv_pend` 0, so not the SVC body's own unlock). It then lets the peer block, and waits until it has blocked.
- Correct code never unlocks `G` at that point, because it goes from a busy scan straight into the wait. The case therefore also requires the wrapper not to have fired.
- A version with a window between the scan and the wait (mutant M9) loses that wakeup every time, and the alarm reports it.
- Before this case was added, M9 was not caught: in 2000 stress rounds the peer's block never landed in the window, about 50 ns wide.

## Open items

- **Host work does not wake the waiter.** `dm_host_work` is a thread-local counter written without `G` (`gfx_enter` / `gfx_leave`, the NVN submit, `dm_host_mutex_lock`, `dm_host_thread_join`). A handoff that ends because the peer enters GPU work is still seen at the 50 µs re-check (the host-work rows, equal to HEAD). Making those paths wake the waiter would need edits in `dm/gfx/nvn/hle_nvn.c` and `dm/host/dm.h`, outside this item. One way: an atomic copy of `g_hand_waiters` read on entry, and a call into `hle_thread.c` that takes `G` and broadcasts only when it is non-zero. Open.
- **The other writes made without `G` rely on the re-check too.** These are `sleeping` from `yield_for` / `dm_guest_sleep_ns`, which in all-peers mode includes a SleepThread with no handoff held; `host_owned` from `dm_thread_mark_host`; and `core` / `mask` from SetThreadCoreMask. None is later than at HEAD.
- **Long handoffs still wake the waiter every 50 µs**, as HEAD's polls did: the waits per round are unchanged. The bound can grow only once host work notifies.
- **`dm_thread_service_request`** is now woken by its starter (revision, item 2). Its 50 µs bound stays only for `thr_free` and a second StartThread.
- **The broadcast runs on the thread that changes state, under `G`.** In `block` it is about 1 µs and 5k instructions per handoff (W in the 0 µs row), and it lengthens that hold of `G` by the same amount. The filter keeps unrelated blocks from paying it.
- **`t_handoff_wake` is not in the Makefile's `TSAN_T` list.** H3 likewise left its test out. It ran under TSan and ASan+UBSan by hand (`san.sh`, above).
- **The catalog's rate is low.** MKW logs 28.7 handoffs a second at the b62 dump, not ~13. DK has no state dump with the line.
- **Not booted.** Boots of the three candidates (muted, through the guard) are the main thread's.
