## What the diff does

1. Every `pthread_cond_signal` on a thread's `cv` that ran under `G` now goes through `cv_signal(t)`: `wake()`, StartThread (0x09) and `dm_guest_clock_advanced`. `cv_signal` appends `t` to a per-host-thread list (`__thread g_cv_defer[16]`, `g_cv_ndefer`) and counts it in a new `thr_t` field `_Atomic int cv_pend`. When the list is full (a 17th wake in one critical section) it signals at once under `G`, as before.
2. `cv_flush()` signals the list in wake order (FIFO), then decrements each thread's `cv_pend` (release). It runs after `G` is released:
   - in `svc_thread`, right after `svc_thread_impl` returns, before the same-core handoff wait (`dm_yield_to_core_peers` waits for the woken thread to block, so it must already be signalled). This covers every SVC that wakes: 0x09, 0x11, 0x19, 0x1B, 0x1C, 0x1D, 0x35;
   - in `thread_exit` after its unlock (it never returns through `svc_thread`);
   - in `dm_guest_clock_advanced` and in the three host-callable signal functions `hle_signal_system_event_binding`, `hle_signal_system_event`, `hle_signal_bound_system_event` (host threads such as audio and vsync never pass through `svc_thread`).
3. Before an implicit release of `G`: `block()` flushes before its first `pthread_cond_wait` / `pthread_cond_timedwait`. That is the only wait on `G` that can follow a wake in the same critical section: WaitProcessWideKeyAtomic hands its lock to a waiter (`lock_handoff` → `wake`) and then blocks. There the signal is still issued with `G` held, as before. Every other wait on `G` starts with an empty list:
   - `thread_main`'s start wait: a fresh pthread that has issued no SVC;
   - `thr_svc_leave`'s pause wait: after `svc_thread` flushed;
   - SetThreadActivity's `pcv` wait: no wake in 0x32, and the list is empty at every SVC entry;
   - `on_pause_sig`: it stops only a thread in guest code, where the list is empty, and it is a signal handler, so it does not touch the thread-local list (the first access of a `__thread` variable may allocate).
4. A signal never reaches a freed thread: `thr_free` (under `G`) waits with `sched_yield` until `cv_pend` is 0 (acquire) before `pthread_cond_destroy` and `free`. The increment is made under `G`, so `thr_free`, which also holds `G`, sees every one. The flusher needs no `G` to finish, so the wait is bounded by its syscall. The caller's own list never holds the thread being freed: `thr_free` runs only inside SVC 0x0A (on the exiting thread) or 0x16, the list is empty at SVC entry, 0x16 wakes nobody, and `thread_exit` wakes only the waiters of the exiting thread.
5. Unchanged: who is woken, in what order, `wres`, lock words, condition keys, `note_runnable` and the handoff bookkeeping. All of it is still decided under `G`; only the host-level signal moves after the release. A deferred signal that arrives after its thread already left the wait (it timed out after being woken, or woke spuriously) and started another wait is a spurious wakeup there; `block` and `thread_main` loop on their condition.
6. Not changed: the `pcv` broadcasts of the pause machinery (`on_pause_sig`, `thr_svc_enter`, `thr_svc_leave`, SetThreadActivity) still run under `G`. They are a different condition variable, used only for collector pauses, and outside this item.

## Darwin: what a signal under the mutex does

Source: Apple's libpthread-539 (`github.com/apple-oss-distributions/libpthread`, commit `42d026df`, 2025-10-16), cloned to `/Volumes/Hangar/Games/overnight/src-H3-bench/apple/libpthread`.

- **No wait morphing.**
  - `_pthread_psynch_cond_signal` calls `__psynch_cvsignal(cond, cvlsgen, ucntval, thread, NULL, 0, 0, flags)` (`src/pthread_cond.c:451`): no mutex is passed.
  - The kernel side declares the mutex arguments unused: `_psynch_cvsignal(..., __unused user_addr_t mutex, __unused uint64_t mugen, ...)` (`kern/kern_synch.c:1156-1166`). It only wakes a waiter of the condition variable's wait queue (`__psynch_cvsignal`, `:1053-1129`).
  - The waiter drops the mutex before it sleeps (`_pthread_mutex_droplock`, `pthread_cond.c:610`; `__psynch_cvwait`, `:629`/`:633`), and on wakeup re-locks it in user space with a plain `pthread_mutex_lock(mutex)` (`:660`). The ulock variant does the same through `_pthread_ulock_cond_wait_complete` → `_pthread_mutex_ulock_lock(mutex, false)` (`:741-747`).
  - So a thread woken while its waker still holds `G` returns to user space and contends for `G` like any other thread.
- **The first-fit mutex does not spin.** `_pthread_mutex_firstfit_lock_slow` (`src/pthread_mutex.c:1419-1481`) makes one CAS; if the E-bit is set it increments L and sleeps in the kernel (`__psynch_mutexwait`, `:1392-1405`). Every thread that touches `G` during a `cvsignal` syscall made under `G` therefore sleeps and must be woken again (the unlocker then pays a `__psynch_mutexdrop` too).
- **This Mac uses the psynch path.**
  - Defaults: first-fit, ulock off (`_PTHREAD_MTX_OPT_POLICY_DEFAULT` FIRSTFIT, `_PTHREAD_MTX_OPT_ULOCK_DEFAULT false`, `src/types_internal.h:179`, `:187`); ulock only through the kernel's registration data or `PTHREAD_MUTEX_USE_ULOCK` (`pthread_mutex.c:137-152`).
  - Probe (`src-H3-bench/probe.c`, Darwin 27.0.0): a `PTHREAD_MUTEX_INITIALIZER` mutex, like `G`, leaves its condition variable signed `0x434F4E45` after a wait, `_PTHREAD_COND_SIG_psynch` (`types_internal.h:105`); with `PTHREAD_MUTEX_USE_ULOCK=1` it is `0x434F4E46` (ulock).
- **Counting switches.** On Darwin `ru_nvcsw` stays 0. `ru_nivcsw` counts every switch, blocks included: the probe's 100 `usleep`s added 103. Mach's `TASK_EVENTS_INFO.csw` gave the same numbers in every run below.

Instrumentation:
- Wrapper macros around `pthread_mutex_lock` / `unlock` / `cond_wait` / `cond_timedwait` / `cond_signal` time each critical section of `G` in the waking SVC (24 MHz counter, 41.7 ns resolution).
- They count explicit acquisitions of `G` that found it taken (a failed `trylock` first), and signals issued with `G` held against after.
- A woken thread's re-acquisition inside `pthread_cond_wait` is invisible to them. It shows in the context switches: process-wide `ru_nivcsw` and task `csw`, per round; the minimum is one block per wait.

What the numbers say:
- **`G` held per waking SVC:** the `cvsignal` syscall (0.4-0.9 µs alone, 0.7-1.6 µs under contention) leaves the critical section. SignalToAddress holds `G` 18-64 ns instead of 0.4-1.8 µs. A four-thread fan-out holds it 0.08 µs instead of 3.9-4.3 µs; eight threads, 0.24 µs instead of 9.6-10.4 µs.
- **Other threads' waits for `G`:** at a realistic rate (4 hammers, 20 µs gap), their SVC p90 falls from 8.9-9.0 µs to 0.21 µs and their mean from 2.5 µs to 0.37-0.38 µs. Explicit acquisitions that found `G` taken fall from 0.45 to 0.15 per round, and switches from 2.51 to 2.12 per round. HEAD's p90 of 9 µs is a full kernel sleep and wakeup on `G`: the first-fit mutex does not spin.
- **The woken thread itself:** in pingpong, HEAD already ran at the minimum of 2.00-2.03 switches a round. At this wake latency (1-2.5 µs) the woken thread seldom reaches `G` before its waker releases it, so the catalog's premise, "a woken thread may wake only to block on `G` again", is rare for one-to-one wakes. It is frequent in fan-outs: with K = 8, HEAD's switches per round are 15.5-16.4 against 11.1, and the first woken thread runs after 13.8-14.5 µs instead of 3.4-3.5 µs.
- **Pingpong wake-to-run:** neutral. Set 1: same median (1,083 ns), p90 1,167 → 1,708-1,958 ns, round +22 %, switches 2.00 → 2.04. Set 2: median 2,292-2,375 → 2,208-2,333 ns, round −3 to −5 %. Both sets lie within the spread between them.
- **Saturated `G`** (2 hammers, no gap, 7-12 M SVC/s): pingpong rounds are 30-50 % slower in set 2 and 30-60 % slower in set 1. The hammers' throughput rises 45 % in set 2 (6.9-7.7 → 10.4-11.1 M/s) and is about equal in set 1. In HEAD the hammers slept in the kernel behind every signal under `G`; now they win the barging first-fit lock more often against the waking pair. It is a share shift at a rate 40-70 times the games'.
- **Not measured:** the effect in a game. That needs a boot, and boots are the main thread's.

## What did not hold, open items

- **The catalog's premise holds only for fan-outs.** The premise is "a woken thread may wake only to block on `G` again". For one-to-one wakes it is rare at Darwin's 1-2.5 µs wake latency, and the pingpong is neutral: set 1 round +22 % with a worse p90; set 2 −3 to −5 %. The gain is `G`'s hold time (about 20 times shorter per waking SVC), the other threads' waits for `G`, and fan-outs.
- **Saturated `G` shifts the share.** With two hammers and no gap, the waking pair is 30-60 % slower and the hammers up to 45 % faster. That rate is 40-70 times the games'. It is not expected in a game, but it was not measured in one.
- **Fixed cost.** Each thread SVC pays +16-44 instructions and 2-5 ns for the thread-local check in `svc_thread`. At MKW's rate that is under 0.1 % of a core.
- **Still under `G`:**
  - the 17th and later wakes of one critical section (the list holds 16; the old behaviour, and correct);
