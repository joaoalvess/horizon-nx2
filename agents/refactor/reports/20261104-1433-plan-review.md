## Verdict

**approve with changes.**

The direction holds: safety net first, verbatim moves judged by a digest, a layer order that is really acyclic, and the inlining and ARC rules taken from probes. As written, though, the plan:
- forfeits the last cheap upstream sync;
- trusts the digest beyond what it hashes;
- has no working regression check for the CPU cost the split adds;
- leaves iOS unchecked per step;
- carries known memory-safety races through every M commit;
- leaves part of goal 1 (`hle_nvn.c`, cleanup, the tests' exit from the unity) without a stage.

Findings 1-7 should change the plan before M0 starts. The rest can be folded in as the stages come up.

## Answers to the brief, in short

1. **Both goals.** Goal 1 is delivered for `nvn_mtl.m`'s structure, not for the layer:
   - `hle_nvn.c` (2,926 lines) has no stage;
   - nothing cleans the code;
   - the 72 white-box tests have no exit from the unity file;
   - `S` / `R` stay writable from about 10 modules.

   Goal 2 comes only with SK / S4 / S5, after 60+ structural commits. M itself adds an unmeasured CPU cost (findings 3, 7).
2. **Criteria and order.** Several criteria are not checkable as written:

   The digest is blind to bindings, buffer contents and pipeline contents (finding 2). The order M0 → M → I → SK → S4 → S5 is safe, with three provisos:
   - S4's design depends on S5's threading design (finding 12);

3. **The split.** It is sound. The matrix is lower-triangular for the edges it models, and the source spot checks hold. But the model leaves out the upward calls into `hle_nvn.c` (finding 7). 4. **D4, D5, D6.**

   - D5 is wrongly argued and must be decided before M step 1.
   - D6's recommendation is right but its rationale is wrong; the real issue is the re-entrancy contract.
5. **Gaps.** Findings 1, 4-9.
6. **Factual errors.** Table at the end.

### 2. The digest does not see bindings, buffer contents or pipeline contents

What `nvn_rec_digest.inc` hashes:
- **Texture and sampler bindings: nothing.** Spec `'i'` (`RO_T_TEX`, `RO_T_SAMP`) hashes a constant: `rdg_u(UINT64_MAX); g_rdg_rids++` ("texture or sampler IDs not named").
- **Buffer contents: nothing.** `'a'` hashes a (buffer, offset) pair (`rdg_addr`), never the bytes. Argument blocks, uniform shadows, `c0` rows and index or indirect data written through the arena's CPU pointer (C1) are invisible.
- **Pipelines and depth-stencil states: only their label.** `rdg_def` hashes nothing else, and `nvn_mtl.m` labels only `PSO_WHOLE` pipelines (`:6311`). A pipeline built from the wrong descriptor, in the same creation order, hashes the same.
- **Not recorded at all:**
  - sampler states;
  - CPU uploads and `replaceRegion`;
  - residency-set changes (`res_add_at`, `res_remove`);
  - post-op writes into guest memory (`post_run`, `:1566`).
- **Unresolved buffers.** An address outside a resident buffer hashes as `?`.
- **Not the direct path.** `--digest` forces `g_rec = g_rdg_sync = 1` (`nvn_replay.m`, uncommitted diff). On the Mac only DK runs the recorder (`dm/games/040074A01BF12000/game.tsv:44`; `dm/games/README.md:203`). So the direct branches Pokopia and MKW take (`:991`, `:998`, `:1014`, `:11561`, `:12956`) are checked only by the pixel replay.

Consequence. A step that does any of these leaves the digest unchanged:
- binds the wrong view or sampler;
- writes a wrong argument or uniform byte;
- builds a pipeline from the wrong key.

Only pixels then catch it, and only if the error reaches the presented frame beyond the capture's noise. The DK opening captures run at `--max-pixels` 3,000-14,000 (`captures.md`).

That is acceptable for verbatim moves. It is not for I (registry ownership, generations), S4 (registry view, program metadata: exactly what feeds the bindings) or SK. Yet the Oracles section says the digest "judges every pure move (M) and every interface step (I)".

**Change.** Before I at the latest, and preferably in S3a/M0:
- name resource IDs through a reverse map (`gpuResourceID` → object, filled where the host takes the ID: `tex_rid_` `:5296`, sampler creation);
- hash each pipeline's key or descriptor at creation (`pso_key_fill`, `pso_desc`), not its label;
- hash argument-block bytes at commit, with each 8-byte word that falls inside a resident buffer replaced by (name, offset) and each resource ID by its name.

Until then:
- list these blind spots under "What the replay does not see";
- make "the diff gets read" concrete: `--digest-dump` output from base and HEAD, run through a differ that normalizes arena offsets.

### 3. M's CPU cost is unmeasured and ungated

Why the cost is real:
- The map breaks the plan's own single-caller rule 168 times (map section 6): 35 callees from `draw_`, 29 from `mtl_exec`, 8 from `encode_args_`, 8 from `rp_begin_ex_`.
- Across modules there is no single-caller bonus (mechanics 3: cost 330 against a threshold of 250 gives a call).

Why the plan cannot see it:
- The oracle cannot resolve it. `commands.md` puts one run at about 5 % on DK and 10 % on Pokopia, and the final run in `captures.md` shows "DK runs of the same build now differ by up to 26 %".
- A few hundred extra instructions per draw, at 1,000-6,000 draws a frame, is about 1-3 % of a 26-67 M-instruction frame. It adds up over 33 steps and stays below that resolution.
- The M row asks for the inlining to be "checked", with no pass/fail rule, and "Measurement is reported, not a gate".
- M0 also changes what is measured. Today `nvn_replay` compiles `nvn_mtl.m` and `hle_nvn.c` into its own TU, and the base replayer (`base-b0d98ee7/nvn_replay_count`) is that unity binary. From M0 on, the replayer links objects, so comparisons against base mix two binaries.

**Change.**
- **(a) A deterministic count.** Add an instruction-count mode: `DM_NVN_UB_COPY_THREAD=0` (removes the `ubc_wait` spin, `nvn_rec.h:40-48`), background work inline (the `--digest` dispatch switch) and a fixed `DM_NVN_ENC_THREAD`. With these the replay thread's count should be nearly deterministic; measure it per step and in total against a pre-M baseline taken at M0.
- **(b) A call check.** Per step, diff the `bl` targets inside `draw_`, `encode_args_`, `tex_fast*`, `rp_begin_ex_` and `rp_end` against the previous step. The allowlist comes from map section 6; any other new call is fixed before the next step. That is a fix, not a drop, so it fits the user's 13:20 decision.
- **(c) A user decision.** Let the user choose: accept the 168 calls, or keep the per-draw single-caller callees in `draw` until I, reading other modules' state through hidden externs.

### 4. iOS is outside the per-step checks, and D5 is argued wrongly

Why iOS is exposed:
- iOS Release is `-Os` with no LTO: `dm/ios/project.yml` sets neither key (mechanics 1.3).
- Every M step therefore turns cross-module helpers into calls on iOS.
- iOS runs the recorder for every game (`nvn_rec.h:82-88`).
- The rec module's helpers that cross modules read `@public` ivars: `rec_table_op` reads `DmRecTable->real`. Read from another module, that costs 5 instructions instead of 2 on iOS (mechanics 4).

What the plan checks:
- iOS appears only in M0 ("the iOS build compiles").
- `compile_check.py` compiles each file `-O1 -w -c -o /dev/null`. It does not link and shows no warnings. A split's typical failures are link failures: a duplicate `@implementation`, a hidden extern missing from iOS.

What is wrong with D5:
- Its rationale, "so iOS inlines across modules as the Mac does", does not hold.
- At `-Os`, LLVM uses its size inline threshold, far below the `-O2` one, so ThinLTO at `-Os` imports and inlines only very small callees.
- iOS also loses the single-caller bonus, like the Mac. The mechanics probes tested only small functions under `-Os` + ThinLTO.

**Change.**
- Decide D5 before M step 1, offering `GCC_OPTIMIZATION_LEVEL = 2` together with `LLVM_LTO = YES_THIN` (the Mac's B1 choice).
- Add an unsigned iOS link of the Release configuration (`build_ipa.sh` or `xcodebuild`, no signing, no install) to every module step, or every third with the boots.
- Hand the user a sideloadable IPA at milestones, since only the user can run it (`CLAUDE.md`).

### 5. M0 changes the build in ways its checks do not cover

- **Per-game objects.** `game_profile.h` is generated per game in `gen/<id>/` (`dm/host/Makefile:16`, included through `-I$(dir $(GAME_H))`). It feeds compile-time constants into the translator: `DM_GAME_ENC_THREAD` (`nvn_rec.h:89`) and `DM_GAME_RENDER_COND` (`:1579`, `:11751`). `GAME_STAMP` (`Makefile:69-74`) only forces the link.
  - With one object directory and `-MMD`, a GAME switch keeps objects compiled for the previous game: their `.d` names the other game's header, which did not change. The host for game B then carries game A's constants, and nothing reports it.
  - **Change:** objects under `gen/<id>/obj/`.
- **`DM_HOST_REV`.** It is a command-line define (`Makefile:80`), read only in `stream_mac.m:145`, the build string the stream sends. Per-object rules never rebuild that object when the revision changes, so the hub sees a stale revision.
  - **Change:** a generated header written only when the revision changes, or a forced dependency for that object.
- **iOS generators.** `gen_build.py:13-28` and `compile_check.py:15-24` take sources, `-I` and `-D` from `make -n -B dmhost`, and sort the line containing `dmhost` first so the host's include paths come before libvpx's. With per-object rules, the `-o dmhost` line has no `-I`, and every compile line repeats the `-D`s.
  - **Change:** adapt both scripts. Criterion: `gen_build.py`'s stdout and the `Gen/src` listing are the same before and after M0, up to order.
- **The replayer also reaches `hle_nvn.c` statics.** The "22 internals" are `nvn_mtl.m`'s, but the replayer also uses:
  - `pk_get` (`hle_nvn.c:638`; `nvn_replay.m:318`);
  - `g_tpb` / `g_spb` / `g_ntpb` / `g_nspb` and `struct dpool_base` (`hle_nvn.c:278`; `nvn_replay.m:320`);
  - `thr_instr` (`hle_nvn.c:1239`; `nvn_replay.m:382`, `:386`).

  "With `hle_nvn.c` linked separately" makes these APIs too.
  - **Change:** list them in M0, or keep `hle_nvn.c` included in the replayer's TU. The replayer measures the submit, not `hle_nvn.c`.
- **Host code changes with no runtime check.** M0 changes host code: the runtime `NVN_REC_TAP`, the `dispatch_async` switch at the 7 sites, statics made hidden extern. Its criterion has no pixel corpus and no boot.
  - **Change:** add the corpus and the three boots to M0.
- **"The suites pass" cannot hold.** `t_gfx_suspend` and `t_nvn` do not link (night Open 04:38; `dm/gfx/tests/Makefile:50-53` lack `t_host_stubs.c`). M0 edits that Makefile in 12 places anyway.
  - **Change:** fix the link there, or name the exclusion.

### 6. Fix the known races now, not in I; root-cause DK's nondeterminism

- **`fb_prepare`'s stale serial.** It has a one-line fix (night Open 10:49): compare `g_treg[tid].t.serial == ser` inside the `:9859` lock.
- **Why now.** Deferring both to I leaves them in every build through 60+ commits. It also makes them non-portable: D1 keeps S0-S2b cherry-pick friendly; fixes made inside modules are not.
- **Change.**
  - Fix both now as S2 items, with tests and `docs/reference` entries (`CLAUDE.md`, "Where a new finding goes").
  - The `DM_NVN_FUSE_CHECK` re-entry abort is diagnostic-only: leave it to I, but write the entry now.
  - `SetThreadActivity`'s use after free is in `hle_thread.c`, the file the HT track owns right now: move it from X to HT.
- **DK's nondeterministic replays (night Open 07:10).** 5 of the 6 DK captures run at `--max-pixels` 100-14,000. In warm replays, the upgrade of ser 4624 at pass 94 changes the output, and the live game crosses frames the same way, so this is likely a host bug. It also threatens S3a's own criterion ("identical across `--repeat` on every capture").
  - **Change:** root-cause it before S3a's baselines (`CLAUDE.md`'s root-cause rule). At least state what DK coverage is lost, and how a capture whose digest varies is handled, instead of letting it drop out silently.

### 10. D6: right recommendation, wrong rationale

- **The rationale.** The "function table … loses inlining" argument does not hold. `draw` (`:10421`) is a one-line wrapper around `draw_` (351 lines), and across modules neither would be inlined either way. A table costs one predictable indirect call per replayed or emulated draw, which is negligible.
- **The real reasons.** For the whitelisted `mtl_replay.h`: direct calls, grep-able, no init order.
- **The real issue: the re-entrancy contract.** `rp_end` → (slot) `fz_end` → `fz_check_run` → `draw_` can run inside `encode_args_` under `g_rmu` (night Open 05:00). `draw_` already copes with a pass ended under it (`:10613-10620`).
  - **Change:** write that contract into `mtl_replay.h`'s note and into the check's whitelist entry.
- **The six init-filled slots.** They have three costs:
  - NULL in any test that never calls `mtl_init`;
  - an init-order dependency;
  - a reader of `rp_end` cannot find who fills the slot.

  **Change:** use link-time `const` tables, defined by the upper module with static initializers, as the hook table already is. That removes the first two, and ThinLTO may fold a hidden `const` table into direct calls (unverified on ld64; a probe settles it).

### 11. The split: sound, with one merge and one regrouping

**What I verified.**
- The matrix is lower-triangular for the modeled edges; the re-run is described at the top.
- Spot checks in the source hold:
  - `rp_end`'s order (`:3886-3901`);
  - `sub_cut`'s reads (`:11540-11566`) and its two callers (`:11794`, `nvn_geometry.inc:799`);

- **Limit.** The pipeline's inputs are the BASE AST mapped to HEAD, without the edges to `hle_nvn.c`, `nvn_dirty.c` and `dm/gfx/tex` (finding 7).

**Granularity.** 12 of 33 modules are under 300 lines. That is acceptable.

**Changes.**

- **Optional:** exec with facade.

**Correction.** "1-3 may swap" is wrong: texfmt reads prof and dev globals (matrix row 3: `d d`), so by the map's own step rule it must follow both.

### 12. Stage criteria to tighten

- **M.** Resolving the per-draw switches "once at init" is a behaviour change inside a verbatim stage: `getenv` moves to init, and the `MLOG` lines move with it.

### 14. Minor
